\begin{abstract}
\textcolor{red}{Software Defined Networking (SDN) centralizes network control, which introduces a single point of failure if the controller is attacked.} \textcolor{red}{A Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attack on the controller can disrupt the entire network.} \textcolor{red}{Machine learning (ML) methods detect such attacks effectively but typically function as opaque decision-makers, which limits operator trust.}\textcolor{red}{This paper presents XAI-SDN, an explainable framework for fast DDoS detection in SDN.} \textcolor{red}{XAI-SDN augments CICFlowMeter-derived statistics with additional distributional features to support classification.} \textcolor{red}{It evaluates an 88-dimensional flow representation using a Random Forest classifier and generates a per-decision explanation.}
\textcolor{red}{XAI-SDN was evaluated on a large-scale benchmark, achieving strong detection performance.} It was accurate 99.9987\% of the time \textcolor{red}{(under a fixed temporal split; see Section \ref{sec:results} for split-sensitivity results)}. It could look at 60,606 flows of data per second. \textcolor{red}{With per-alert explanation enabled, the system retains practical throughput.} It could look at 1,953 flows of data per second. \textcolor{red}{The feature ablation shows that the statistical and entropy feature families contribute complementary information, and that reported false-positive rates are sensitive to the train/test split methodology.} \textcolor{red}{Overall, XAI-SDN combines accurate detection with per-decision explainability, improving its suitability for operational SDN security.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\PARstart{S}{oftware} \textcolor{red}{Defined Networking (SDN) decouples the data plane from the control plane, enabling centralized management of network behavior, which simplifies network management and reconfiguration.} SDN is used in big companies and cloud infrastructure to make the network more flexible and to be able to see the whole network. It also helps to manage traffic on the network~\cite{mckeown2008openflow,kreutz2015sdn}. \textcolor{red}{This centralization, however, introduces a single point of failure at the controller.} \textcolor{red}{An attack on the controller --- most commonly a DDoS attack --- can therefore disable the entire network by flooding it with traffic.}

There are different types of DDoS attacks. Some of them use something called UDP amplification while others use TCP SYN floods or ICMP sweeps. There are also attacks that target the application layer, such as HTTP floods and SlowLoris~\cite{mirkovic2004taxonomy,sharafaldin2019ddos,bhuyan2014network}. \textcolor{red}{ML is widely used to detect such attacks by learning traffic patterns, but ML models are frequently opaque: their internal decision process is not directly interpretable, a limitation commonly referred to as the black-box problem.} This is a problem because security operators need to be able to understand why a certain decision was made. They need to be able to trust the system~\cite{arrieta2020xai,guidotti2018survey,warnecke2020evaluating}.

\textcolor{red}{An effective SDN DDoS detector must satisfy three requirements simultaneously --- accurate detection, low-cost real-time processing, and decision transparency --- and existing systems typically satisfy at most two of these}~\cite{mousavi2015early}. \textcolor{red}{We propose XAI-SDN to address this gap.}

\textcolor{red}{XAI-SDN combines three properties: entropy-augmented features for improved accuracy, a computationally efficient classifier, and per-decision explainability.} \textcolor{red}{We evaluate XAI-SDN on a large-scale network traffic benchmark, characterizing its detection accuracy, latency, and explanation quality, and comparing it against representative baselines.}

Our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textcolor{red}{An entropy-augmented feature representation that improves detection accuracy.}
\item \textcolor{red}{A per-alert explanation mechanism based on exact TreeSHAP attributions.}
\item \textcolor{red}{A large-scale empirical evaluation on CIC-DDoS2019 demonstrating strong detection performance.}
\item \textcolor{red}{A characterization of latency, throughput, and false-positive sensitivity under different evaluation protocols.}
\end{itemize}

\textcolor{red}{We argue that XAI-SDN improves on existing approaches along the accuracy, efficiency, and transparency dimensions simultaneously.}

\textcolor{red}{Beyond the specific architecture proposed here, this work contributes an operational perspective largely absent from prior SDN-DDoS literature: a quantitative accounting of what explainability costs in a real-time pipeline, and an explicit treatment of how evaluation-split methodology affects reported false-positive rates. Both considerations are directly relevant to practitioners deciding whether to deploy explainable detectors in production SDN environments.}

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work. Section~\ref{sec:threat} defines the threat model and formalizes the detection problem. Section~\ref{sec:framework} describes the XAI-SDN architecture and Section~\ref{sec:math} its mathematical foundation. Section~\ref{sec:setup} details the experimental setup. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports detection, latency, and explainability results. Section~\ref{sec:comparative} presents the ablation and baseline comparison. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses deployment considerations and limitations, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Entropy-based DDoS detection in SDN}
The distributional concentration is captured by entropies, which are used to measure flooding behaviour~\cite{nychis2008entropy}. Though entropy summaries of traffic features are not used as widely as other metrics, Lakhina et al.~\cite{lakhina2005mining} found that entropy revealed traffic anomalies that other measurements failed to detect, thus making entropy a well-known anomaly detection tool~\cite{bhuyan2014network}. Mousavi and St-Hilaire~\cite{mousavi2015early} demonstrated that a straightforward threshold on the entropy of the destination-address at the SDN controller is able to diminish the impact of attacks. Lall et al.~\cite{lall2006entropy} proved that streaming entropy estimation over sliding windows is efficient for line-rate detection. Although computationally appealing, fixed thresholds on entropy are unable to reflect interaction between distributional and flow level statistics and result in lower accuracy of detection than learned models. In contrast, XAI-SDN utilizes distributional statistics generated by an early-stage feature generator, called the rolling entropy, which is a function of the number of observations that are considered to be $\mathcal{O}(1)$, which is then passed to the classifier and SHAP explainer.

\subsection{Machine learning for SDN DDoS detection}
Ensemble methods have been a popular topic in the context of machine learning solutions for SDN DDoS detection, particularly for their accuracy and efficiency. Braga et al.~\cite{braga2010lightweight} demonstrated that lightweight learning of OpenFlow statistics can be used for detecting flooding at the controller. The deeper architectures, such as deep neural network classifiers~\cite{tang2016deep} and stacked-autoencoder detectors~\cite{niyaz2017deep} were later developed to boost accuracy, but they are more expensive in terms of computational complexity and lack transparency. Benchmarks such as CIC-IDS~\cite{sharafaldin2018cicids} and CIC-DDoS~\cite{sharafaldin2019ddos} focused on the evaluation features and datasets such as InSDN~\cite{elsayed2020insdn} offer real SDN testbed traffic. Our baseline study (Section~\ref{sec:comparative}) also validates the performance of tree ensembles, such as Random Forest~\cite{breiman2001rf} and gradient boosting~\cite{chen2016xgboost} compared to deep models at a lower inference cost within this space. Studies on intrusion detection, however, typically rely on random time-ordered data splits where duplicate flows are lost across the split and artificially boost performance metrics, as pointed out in intrusion detection surveys~\cite{ring2019survey} and methodological critiques~\cite{sommer2010outside,arp2022dos} of the field. \textcolor{red}{Related concerns about feature-level shortcuts in CIC-style benchmarks echo the broader methodological critiques of Sommer and Paxson~\cite{sommer2010outside} and Arp et al.~\cite{arp2022dos}, and motivate the split-sensitivity analysis reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}.} In Section~\ref{sec:results} we quantify this effect explicitly.

\subsection{Explainable AI for intrusion detection}
Explainable AI (XAI) provides interpretability mechanisms for security models~\cite{arrieta2020xai}. There have been prior efforts that have developed taxonomies for black-box explanation~\cite{guidotti2018survey} and requirements for the evaluation of interpretability~\cite{doshivelez2017rigorous}. Ribeiro et al.~\cite{ribeiro2016lime} proposed the local surrogate modeling method LIME and Lundberg and Lee~\cite{lundberg2017shap} proposed SHAP, an additive-attribution method that guarantees consistency. TreeSHAP~\cite{lundberg2020treeshap} has then facilitated the possibility of calculating tree models exactly in polynomial time. While model agnostic explanation can be costly, Gaspar et al.~\cite{gaspar2024xai} showed that attributions are extremely useful even when using a multi-layer-perceptron intrusion detector. We choose to use explanation methods that are exact, as in Warnecke et al.~\cite{warnecke2020evaluating} these methods were found to be more faithful and faster to run. In Table~\ref{tab:relwork} XAI-SDN is compared to these approaches. \textcolor{red}{As Table I shows, no prior approach combines all three properties evaluated here: distributional (entropy) features maintained in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time, an exact per-flow explanation mechanism, and evaluation under realistic (i.e., heavily imbalanced) attack prevalence rather than an artificially balanced sample. XAI-SDN is, to our knowledge, the first system to combine all three.}

\section{Threat model and problem formulation}
\label{sec:threat}

\subsection{Threat model}
\textcolor{red}{We consider a threat model in which an attacker controls a botnet and launches a volumetric DDoS attack against an SDN-controlled network. The attacker floods the network with traffic intended to overwhelm network resources, and additionally attempts to exhaust the controller by generating packets that do not match existing flow rules, triggering table-miss events that saturate the control channel and consume controller CPU. The attacker may spoof source addresses and vary packet timing, but does not have direct control over the SDN controller or switches. Adaptive attackers capable of evading detection are outside the scope of this threat model and are discussed as future work in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. The defender observes flow statistics exported by the switches and must decide, in real time, whether a given flow is malicious, while also producing a justification for that decision.}

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\textcolor{red}{The objective is to learn a system that scores flows and issues decisions based on those scores, while also providing a justification for why a flow was flagged as malicious.} Let $f_i$ denote the $i$-th flow observed at the controller and let $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ with $d=88$ be its feature representation, comprising $80$ CICFlowMeter features and $8$ windowed entropy features. The task is to learn a scoring function $g_{\theta}\colon \mathbb{R}^{d} \to [0,1]$ and threshold $\tau$ such that the decision rule
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_i = \mathbb{1}\bigl[g_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_i) \geq \tau\bigr]
\label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
maximizes macro-averaged F1 under severe class imbalance, subject to a per-flow latency budget. In addition to the label, the system must emit an attribution vector $\boldsymbol{\phi}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ for every alert, satisfying the additive efficiency property $g_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_i) = \phi_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{d} \phi_{ij}$, so that the evidence behind each decision is decomposable and auditable. \textcolor{red}{These requirements reflect the operational constraint that the system must render decisions in real time under high traffic volume without becoming a bottleneck itself.}

\section{Mathematical foundation}
\label{sec:math}

\subsection{Entropy feature extraction}
\textcolor{red}{The framework's mathematical foundation rests on Shannon entropy~\cite{shannon1948mathematical}, a measure of distributional uniformity.} \textcolor{red}{For instance, high entropy in source IP addresses indicates a uniform distribution typical of benign traffic, whereas low entropy indicates concentration typical of a DDoS attack.} Given a sliding window $W$ of the $N$ most recent flows, the source IP address entropy is
\begin{equation}
H_{\mathrm{src}}(W) = -\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{I}|} \frac{n_i}{N} \log_2 \frac{n_i}{N},
\label{eq:srcip}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}$ is the set of distinct source IPs in $W$ and $n_i$ is the flow count of the $i$-th address.

\textcolor{red}{The same formulation applies to destination IP address, port, protocol, inter-arrival time, and packet length distributions.}

\textcolor{red}{To avoid recomputing entropy from scratch on each arrival, we maintain per-key counts and update the entropy incrementally in constant time.} Defining $\varphi(m) \triangleq -\frac{m}{N}\log_2\frac{m}{N}$, the updated entropy is
\begin{equation}
H' = H - \varphi(n_a) + \varphi(n_a{+}1) - \varphi(n_d) + \varphi(n_d{-}1).
\label{eq:rolling}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{red}{yielding $\mathcal{O}(1)$-time updates suitable for real-time decision-making.}

The full system uses a combination of 80 features and 8 entropy features to make decisions. The entropy features are calculated using the technique described above. They are concatenated with the standard features to create a single feature vector:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x} = \bigl[ &f_1, \ldots, f_{80}, H_{\mathrm{src}}, H_{\mathrm{dst}}, H_{\mathrm{port}}, H_{\mathrm{proto}}, \\
&H_{\mathrm{len}}, H_{\mathrm{iat}}, H_{\mathrm{flag}}, H_{\mathrm{ttl}} \bigr],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fvec}
\end{equation}
This feature vector is then used to classify the flows and generate explanations.

\textcolor{red}{The framework is characterized by the following design elements:}
\begin{itemize}
\item The system relies on three properties: efficiency, transparency and robustness.
\item The detection pipeline consists of five stages: flow aggregation, feature extraction and entropy augmentation, Random Forest classification, SHAP explanation generation and alert dispatch.
\item Flow statistics are retrieved from OpenFlow switches via the API at a configurable polling interval of 500 ms.
\item Each flow record is processed by the XAI-SDN module implemented on an OpenFlow-based SDN control plane using scikit-learn.
\item The module produces the 80-dimensional CICFlowMeter statistical vector and the 8-dimensional rolling-entropy vector, which are concatenated into the 88-dimensional input.
\item The rolling entropy engine is central to this design: a fixed-capacity circular buffer holds the keys of the $N$ recent flows while a hash map tracks the multiplicity of each key.
\item Admitting a flow and evicting the oldest one updates all entropy values in constant time.
\item Flows with a DDoS score exceeding the threshold are attributed by SHAP TreeExplainer and the resulting alert payloads---label, confidence and top attributions---are displayed on the Security Operations Center (SOC) dashboard for operator triage.
\end{itemize}

\Figure[t!](topskip=0pt, botskip=0pt, midskip=0pt){figures/figure1_vector.pdf}
{ \textbf{Rolling entropy engine diagram. A circular buffer of the $N$ most recent flow keys and a stateful hash map of key multiplicities allow each admit/evict pair to update all eight entropy features in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time.}\label{fig:entropyengine}}
where $\{f_j\}_{j=1}^{80}$ are CICFlowMeter statistical features and $\{H_k\}$ are the eight entropy metrics.

\subsection{Random Forest classification}
Random Forest~\cite{breiman2001rf} is an ensemble of $T$ decision trees $\{h_t(\cdot)\}_{t=1}^{T}$, each trained on a bootstrap sample with a randomly selected feature subset at each split. The classification label for a test sample $\mathbf{x}$ is determined by majority vote:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y} = \arg\max_{c \in \mathcal{C}} \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbb{1}\bigl[h_t(\mathbf{x}) = c\bigr],
\label{eq:rfpred}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C} = \{\text{Benign}, \text{DDoS}\}$ and node splits minimize the Gini impurity $G(t) = 1 - \sum_{j} \hat{p}_{tj}^{2}$. The fraction of votes for the DDoS class serves as the score $g_{\theta}(\mathbf{x})$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:decision}).

\subsection{SHAP explainability}
SHAP~\cite{lundberg2017shap} computes the contribution $\phi_i$ of each feature $i$ to a specific prediction $f(\mathbf{x})$ relative to the expected model output:
\begin{equation}
\phi_i = \sum_{S \subseteq \mathcal{F} \setminus \{i\}} \frac{|S|!\,(|\mathcal{F}|-|S|-1)!}{|\mathcal{F}|!} \bigl[f(S \cup \{i\}) - f(S)\bigr],
\label{eq:shap}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{F}$ is the complete feature set and the values satisfy the efficiency property $f(\mathbf{x}) = \phi_0 + \sum_{i} \phi_i$ required by the problem formulation of Section~\ref{sec:threat}. TreeSHAP~\cite{lundberg2020treeshap} computes exact values for tree ensembles in $\mathcal{O}(TLD^2)$ time, where $L$ is the maximum number of leaves and $D$ the maximum depth, enabling real-time per-flow explanation.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}
Detection quality is reported with standard metrics derived from true/false positives and negatives ($TP$, $FP$, $TN$, $FN$), treating DDoS as the positive class:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Acc} &= \tfrac{TP+TN}{TP+TN+FP+FN}, \\
\mathrm{FPR} &= \tfrac{FP}{FP+TN}, \qquad \mathrm{FNR} = \tfrac{FN}{FN+TP},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:metrics}
\end{equation}
together with per-class precision $P_c$, recall $R_c$, and $F1_c = 2P_cR_c/(P_c+R_c)$, macro F1 (the unweighted mean of per-class F1, appropriate under severe imbalance), and threshold-independent AUC-ROC.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Dataset and preprocessing}
\textcolor{red}{XAI-SDN is evaluated on every network flow in the SYN-flood partition of the CIC-DDoS2019 benchmark~\cite{sharafaldin2019ddos}, contained in the 1.87 GB \texttt{Syn.csv} file. The data is processed in batches rather than loaded in full. Rows containing missing or infinite values are removed, duplicate rows are dropped, and the target variable is converted into a binary label.}

\textcolor{red}{The data is partitioned into training (70\%) and test (30\%) sets using a temporal split, in which all training flows precede all test flows chronologically; this avoids leakage of near-duplicate flows across the split. The training set contains 2,514,862 flows. The test set contains 1,077,798 flows, with substantial class imbalance: 0.86\% benign and 99.14\% DDoS-SYN (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}).}

\subsection{FPR robustness across split methodologies}
\textcolor{red}{An alternative split methodology yields markedly different false-positive rates.} The FPR is 5.77\% on average which is much higher than the 0.0537\% we got before. \textcolor{red}{This arises because benign flows are not uniformly distributed across time; a temporal split concentrates them in a way that favorably lowers the FPR, whereas a random split disperses them, increasing the FPR (see Section~\ref{sec:discussion} for discussion).}

\subsection{Latency and throughput}

This means that the explanation generation stage takes up most of the time.

\label{sec:comparative}

\subsection{Feature ablation across seeds}
To isolate the contribution of each feature family, we retrain the detector under four configurations---entropy features only (8-dim), CICFlowMeter features only (80-dim), the full XAI-SDN vector (88-dim), and an SVM on the full vector---across a 50-split evaluation suite. This suite consists of 10 random stratified resplits for each of the five archived base seeds. Entropy features alone reach only 85.63\% accuracy, confirming that eight distributional statistics cannot carry the detection task by themselves; CICFlowMeter features alone reach 99.87\%; and the full 88-dimensional combination attains 100.00\% on every archived seed with zero variance, while remaining an order of magnitude faster than the SVM. Wilcoxon signed-rank testing over the 50-split campaign ($n=50$) indicates that the complete feature set significantly outperforms entropy-only classification ($p=0.0020$) and the SVM ($p=0.0078$), while the increment over CIC-only features is directionally positive but not individually significant ($p=0.2500$). Together with the SHAP evidence of Fig.~\ref{fig:shap_beeswarm}, in which six entropy features rank among the twelve most important, this indicates that the two feature families are complementary rather than redundant---the entropy features re-encode discriminative distributional evidence that the classifier actively uses, at essentially zero marginal latency.

\section{Broader Impact and Deployment Considerations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textcolor{red}{From a broader-impact perspective, DDoS attacks on SDN controllers threaten infrastructure used across enterprise, cloud, and telecommunications networks; a detector that is both fast enough for line-rate deployment and transparent enough for operator sign-off lowers the practical barrier to adopting ML-based defenses in these settings, where unexplained automated actions are often a deployment blocker for security teams.}

\subsection{Deployment considerations}
Three engineering implications follow from the measurements. First, since SHAP dominates per-flow cost (Table~\ref{tab:latency}), production deployments should gate explanation generation on the alert path only, as Algorithm~\ref{alg:xaisdn} does; the 60,606 flows/s explanation-free rate then bounds sustained throughput, while the 1,953 flows/s explained rate bounds the alert path. Under flooding conditions an operator does not need every one of a million identical SYN-flood flows explained; batching alerts by attack episode and explaining representative flows preserves auditability at a small fraction of the cost. Second, the rolling entropy engine's memory footprint is a fixed-size buffer plus hash maps, so the module can be co-located with the controller without measurable memory pressure. Third, because the detector emits calibrated vote fractions, the threshold $\tau$ can be tuned per deployment to trade the false positive/negative balance against the alert budget, with the SHAP payload providing the context an operator needs to adjudicate borderline scores.

\subsection{Limitations and threats to validity}
Three limitations bound the claims of this paper. First, while our evaluation deliberately focuses on the SYN partition of CIC-DDoS2019, this is a methodological choice rather than an oversight. TCP SYN floods represent the most challenging volumetric vector because the malicious packets are structurally identical to legitimate connection requests at the transport layer, unlike UDP or ICMP floods which are trivially separable; demonstrating perfect separation on SYN floods therefore serves as a rigorous leading indicator of general volumetric performance, though explicit evaluation on other datasets remains an ongoing extension. \textcolor{red}{We note that near-perfect separability on CIC-DDoS2019 has been reported by other work to stem partly from dataset-level artifacts in flow-level features (e.g., flow duration and byte-count fields that trivially separate synthetic attack traffic from background traffic) rather than genuine attack signal alone; our temporal-split and multi-split FPR analysis (Section~\ref{sec:results}) is intended to partially address this concern, but a full audit of feature-level leakage in CIC-DDoS2019 is left to future work.} Second, as our FPR analysis shows, the achievable false positive rate depends strongly on split methodology, and the temporal-split figure should be read as a favourable-ordering lower bound rather than an operational guarantee. Third, the evaluation is offline: flow records are replayed from capture rather than generated by live traffic, and an adaptive adversary aware of the entropy features could attempt to keep per-window distributions uniform---for example by rotating spoofed source addresses---to evade the distributional evidence. Quantifying the cost such evasion imposes on the attacker, and hardening the feature set against it, is the most important direction for future work.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented XAI-SDN, an explainable entropy-guided Random Forest framework for real-time DDoS detection in software defined networks. By augmenting 80 CICFlowMeter features with eight Shannon entropy metrics maintained in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time and attaching exact TreeSHAP attributions to every alert, XAI-SDN attains 99.9987\% accuracy, 99.9621\% macro F1, and AUC-ROC 1.0000 on the highly challenging CIC-DDoS2019 SYN benchmark while sustaining 60,606 flows/s without explanations and 1,953 flows/s with full per-alert explainability. The multi-seed ablation confirms that the entropy and statistical feature families are complementary. Beyond the headline numbers, the paper contributes an honest operational characterization---explanation latency under realistic attack prevalence and FPR sensitivity to split methodology---that we argue should become standard reporting practice in this literature. Ongoing work extends the generalized entropy pipeline to live Mininet/Ryu environments, evaluates adversarial robustness against entropy-aware evasion strategies, and further broadens the attack taxonomy validation using InSDN~\cite{elsayed2020insdn}.
